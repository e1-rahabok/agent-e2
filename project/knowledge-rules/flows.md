# Flows: health checks, troubleshooting, decision trees

Part of the knowledge rules; overview and why: [KNOWLEDGE-ENGINEERING.md](../../KNOWLEDGE-ENGINEERING.md). For writers and reviewers. Not indexed.

- Location: [placement.md](placement.md), placement rules: a flow sits at its entry point, and hands off to another flow at an object or realm boundary.
- Flow is the general word; health check, troubleshooting and decision tree are its three kinds ([glossary](../../knowledge/glossary.md), entry Flow).
- Vocabulary:
  - Framework: how IT operations are managed (SP5). [`sp5-framework/concepts/overview`](../../knowledge/sp5-framework/concepts/overview.md).
  - Model: how one pillar of one object is quantified into a number and a decision (capacity model, availability model, security model). Files in `vsphere/object-types/<object>/models/` (or the object folder while there is one) and `sp5-framework/capacity/`.
  - KPI: a model of performance only (the P in KPI). Files `vsphere/object-types/<object>/<object>-performance`, plus [`guest-os/system/guest-os-performance`](../../knowledge/guest-os/system/guest-os-performance.md).
  - Health check: the what. Is the object healthy, at risk, or wasteful? Always on one object type (VM, host, cluster, datastore), never on a technology (CPU, memory) and never on one pillar. A parent's health includes its children's, unless a realm separates them: Guest OS slowness is not ESXi or VM health, because the hypervisor neither sees nor controls it. Its order of checks is below; the health grouping (present health, risk, efficiency) defines the verdicts, not the order ([`sp5-framework/concepts/health-risk-efficiency`](../../knowledge/sp5-framework/concepts/health-risk-efficiency.md)). Monitoring belongs here. Explained to the agent in [`sp5-framework/concepts/health-check`](../../knowledge/sp5-framework/concepts/health-check.md).
  - Troubleshooting: the why, in the reactive process. It starts from a complaint, an alert, or a failed health check step. Two types only: down (availability) and slow (performance). A security breach gets a forensic investigation, not troubleshooting. Each type uses the same order of checks as its pillar in the health check.
  - Two processes: reactive (troubleshooting, forensic investigation) and proactive (the health check, daily, with weekly and monthly analysis). The health check is a superset of reactive, because nobody knows yet whether there is a problem. Proactive findings arrive as insight, typically anomaly detection read with business context.
  - Decision tree: answers one operational question (can this cluster take more VMs, should this VM be downsized) and ends in a choice to act, with its reason. Not a health check, even when a health check step reads its result. Not a decision record: a decision records what was done; a decision tree decides what to do.
  - Most operational questions are neither a health check nor troubleshooting. A flow for one is a decision tree; a question answered by reading, not by a branching order of checks, is a concept or metric file.
  - Not used: diagnosis (troubleshooting), what to monitor (health check), verification (health check).
- A health check step names the KPI or model, its band, and the flow to open when it fails: `→ <troubleshooting id>`.
- Order: is it up (availability: log, saturation, configuration, known bug), is it fast (performance, the Triple See Method: contention, consumption, then context; context is typically configuration, known bugs included), is it safe (security: configuration, known bug, log), will it be enough (capacity: consumption, configuration, contention, inventory). Within each pillar the what comes before the why: establish the fact, then explain it; do not jump to a conclusion. Known bugs are checked per pillar, because a bug can cause an outage, slowness or a security loophole. Efficiency is part of capacity. Contention is part of performance. Every performance troubleshooting flow checks configuration.
- The health check reads VCF Operations KPIs and models. It does not fall back to vSphere alone: VCF always includes VCF Operations, and other health check tools are outside the scope of Agent E2.
- One flow per file. Template: [`project/templates/health-check.md`](../templates/health-check.md), `troubleshooting.md` or `decision-tree.md`. Order: purpose (health check: nothing is wrong yet, so no symptom), symptom (troubleshooting) or question (decision tree), Mermaid flowchart, numbered steps (`## Checks, in order`), order rationale, interpretation, misconceptions, metadata.
- The Mermaid chart is the master of the flow's structure: its steps, branches and order. Branches are reviewed visually; reading lines of text while imagining the flow is error prone.
  - Every flow has one Mermaid flowchart. The author edits the chart (in Mermaid Live Editor or in the file); Claude then updates the numbered steps to match. Never the reverse: a change Claude proposes to a flow's structure is made in the chart first.
  - The numbered steps are what the LLM follows. The harness leaves the chart out of the model's text ([`ARCHITECTURE.md`](../../ARCHITECTURE.md), section Ingest), so a fact only in the chart never reaches the model. The steps add what a box cannot hold: exact metric names, thresholds with their source, reasons.
  - Lint (`rules_check.py`) fails when chart and steps differ: a step without a box, a box without a step, an arrow from step A to step B (directly, or through boxes that are not steps, such as another flow) without `→ step B` in step A, or the reverse. A step with no `→ step N` ends the flow.
  - A hand-off to another flow (`→ <flow id>` in a step) is a box in the chart with a Mermaid click line that opens that flow on GitHub: `click T3 "https://github.com/e1-rahabok/agent-e2/blob/main/knowledge/<id>.md" "<title>" _blank`. Box text: the flow's title. A dotted arrow (`-.->`) marks a flow that is read or opened but does not return to this one. Lint fails on a hand-off without its click line, and on a click line no step hands off to. GitHub opens the link.
  - Steps as data (Q166) follow the chart the same way: the `then` targets equal its arrows.
- Each step states: the metric (exact name, matching the metric file title), the object, the threshold with its source, and the next step for each outcome.
  - Example: 1. Check VM CPU Ready (VirtualMachine). Above <threshold> (<source>) → step 2. Otherwise → step 4.
- Every metric or flow named in a step is linked in `internal_derive`, so retrieval brings in the files with the flow.
- Steps as data (Q166; to build). The numbered step stays the text the model reads. A `steps` list in the flow's `## Metadata` holds what code needs, one entry per step that code can compare:
  - `step`: the step number.
  - `object`: the object type.
  - `metric`: a metric file title, so it resolves through the catalog.
  - `test`: a catalog row id (metric, object, interval, use), or a comparison of two case values (vCPUs against physical cores). Never a number typed in the flow.
  - `then`: each band or outcome, and its next step.
  - A step code cannot compare (logs, the application team's reading, a known bug) has no entry; the model reads it.
  - Lint checks that each entry matches its numbered step: the metric is named in the text, the targets equal the `→ step N` arrows.
  - First flow: [`vm-cpu-contention-high`](../../knowledge/vsphere/technology/cpu/troubleshooting/vm-cpu-contention-high.md) (steps 1, 3, 4 and 6 compare numbers). VM slow follows as the router: its step 4 picks the sub-flow from the counter that pulls VM Performance (%) down. Why not VM slow first: one numeric step; the rest is the application team, breadth, configuration, bugs and logs.
  - A value the case lacks: in a conversation the agent asks for it (one round, [harness/AGENT.md](../../harness/AGENT.md), 4.4). In a single-turn answer code stops at that step, and the model states both branches and what to read.
- A step's box starts with the step key: `S1[1. VM CPU Ready above threshold?]`, or `Sa[a. Ballooning]` in a flow with lettered steps. Boxes without a key are outcomes or other flows. Step keys tie the chart to the text.
- Keep each flowchart to about 15 nodes. Split a larger flow into linked flow files.
- draw.io is not the master: lint cannot read its XML (mostly layout and style; compressed files are unreadable). A draw.io flow of the author's is converted to Mermaid, and the Mermaid chart is edited from then on.
