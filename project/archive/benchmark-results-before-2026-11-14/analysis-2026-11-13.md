# Analysis of the round of 2026-11-13

What the numbers in [README.md](README.md) mean, why the release gate failed, what was changed, and what to do next. Every number comes from the four run folders of this round; the scripts are in `benchmark/runner/report.py` (functions `final`, `consensus`).

## 1. Verdict
- Is the mission possible: section 11. Real-world Level 400: section 12.
- **The knowledge base works.** It lifts every local model by 19-28 points, well outside the margin (±9). Confident-wrong answers drop by half.
- **A 30B local model with Agent E2 is level with the frontier model.** Muse-glimmer-30b in agentic mode scores 78%; Claude Opus 5.5 with web search 81%. The gap of 3 points is inside the margin.
- **Not releasable yet.** No model passes the release gate. The largest miss is confident-wrong: 20% against a limit of 3%.
- **The frontier model fails the gate too.** Opus web: final 81% (limit 85%), Level 400 79% (limit 80%), confident-wrong 12% (limit 3%). Model size alone does not pass this gate (section 6).

| Model | Baseline | Classic | Agentic | Lift, agentic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen3.8-27b | 41% | 69% | 63% | +23 |
| gemma-4-31b-it | 57% | 78% | 76% | +19 |
| muse-glimmer-30b | 54% | 77% | **78%** | +24 |
| claude-opus-5.5 (no knowledge base) | 84% | - | - | web 81% |

## 2. Where the confident-wrong answers come from
Confident wrong = an answer an operator would act on that is wrong: wrong or partly right letters, or a judged answer scored 0 that does not say the question is not settled.

| Model, mode | All | Multiple choice | Of those, partly right | Unsure chosen | Judged (open) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemma, agentic | 21% | 14% | 67% | 1% | 31% |
| muse, agentic | 20% | 12% | 73% | 1% | 32% |
| qwen, agentic | 30% | 22% | 75% | 6% | 41% |
| gemma, classic | 19% | 10% | 59% | 1% | 33% |
| opus, web | 12% | 5% | 81% | 4% | 23% |

Three findings:
1. **The models do not abstain.** Unsure is chosen in 1-6% of answers, though the prompt says a wrong answer is worse than Unsure. A model does not know what it does not know.
2. **Most wrong letters are partly right.** The model gets the main option and adds or misses one more. The same question gets different letters in different runs: luck is 48-63%.
3. **Open answers carry most of the rate.** A judged answer of up to 250 words scores 0 when one sentence contradicts a key point (`benchmark/runner/judge.py`, rubric). Judged confident-wrong is 31-41% for the local models and 16-23% for Opus.

## 3. Change 1: consensus (built)
- Multiple choice, classic and agentic mode: the harness takes up to 3 samples. Letters stand only when all agree. Otherwise the answer is Unsure.
- Why it works: finding 2. Disagreement between samples is the signal the model cannot give about itself.
- Effect, projected exactly from the 5 recorded runs (every draw of 3 of 5):

| Model, mode | Final | Confident wrong, all | Multiple choice, confident wrong | Multiple choice, Unsure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemma, agentic | 76% → 71% | 21% → 15% | 14% → 4% | 1% → 18% |
| muse, agentic | 78% → 73% | 20% → 15% | 12% → 3% | 1% → 18% |
| qwen, agentic | 63% → 49% | 30% → 16% | 22% → 0% | 6% → 52% |
| gemma, classic | 78% → 75% | 19% → 15% | 10% → 3% | 1% → 13% |
| muse, classic | 77% → 73% | 21% → 17% | 12% → 5% | 2% → 14% |

- Trade-off: about 5 points of final score for two thirds fewer wrong letters. Unsure is not wrong: the operator is told the knowledge base does not settle it.
- Qwen loses 14 points: its agent gives the same letters in all 5 runs on only 35% of the questions.
- Not fixed: open answers (finding 3). The overall rate stays near 15%.
- Code and settings: `harness/modes.py` (`_consensus`), `consensus_samples` in `benchmark/runner/config.yaml`. Design: [harness/README.md](../../harness/README.md), section 6a. Every future `report.md` shows the projection (section Consensus of 3, projected).

## 4. Change 2: read reminder (built, small expected effect)
- Found but not read: in agentic mode a needed file was in the search results but never opened in 22-43% of answers.
- Same question, found-and-not-read against read: muse +5 points, gemma and qwen about 0. The lever is weaker than it looked in the summary.
- Built anyway because it is cheap: before the answer, one reminder names the top search hits the agent did not read (at most 3). Switch `agent_read_reminder`, on for the next round as its one agentic change.
- Code: `harness/agent.py` (`_unread`, `READ_TOP`, `READ_REMIND`). Design: [harness/AGENT.md](../../harness/AGENT.md), section 5.

## 6. Agentic against classic
- Agentic and classic are within the margin for every model (-6, -3, +1 points). Agentic uses 2-3.5 times the tokens and about 2 times the latency.
- This does not make the harness a waste. Classic mode is the harness too: hybrid search, term map, link expansion, catalog facts, troubleshooting routing, the verifier. That is where the +20 points come from.
- What it shows: the agent's own tool use does not add yet. Its gain is on Level 400 chains across files; its loss is unstable lookups (qwen agentic consistency 35%).
- Recommendation: keep agentic as the shipped mode only if the next round shows it ahead of classic; otherwise ship classic with consensus, agentic as an option. This changes an earlier decision (Q130: agentic is the shipped mode and carries the gate), so it is raised as Q200 in `project/author-questions.md`.

## 7. 30B or 70B?
- **No reason in this data to drop the 30B class.**
  - The knowledge base adds 19-28 points to a 30B model. Nothing suggests a 70B model adds as much on its own.
  - A model many times larger, Opus, also fails all three gates, and its confident-wrong rate is 11-12%. Size lowers the rate; it does not reach 3%.
  - The blocker is abstention and open-answer precision. Consensus and answer shape fix those for any size.
- **But test a 70B when one exists.** The plan already names a current-generation dense vision model of about 70B as the first challenger (`project/plan.md`, M4). It is one more run folder on the same 196 questions.
  - On one GPU, a 70B at 4-bit needs about 40 GB for weights plus the KV cache: a 48 GB card is tight at a 32K window, 80 GB is comfortable. A 30B at 4-bit fits a 24 GB card.
  - Decision rule: switch only if the 70B in the shipped mode beats the best 30B by more than the margin (about ±9 points) or halves confident-wrong. A tie keeps the smaller model (`benchmark/design.md`, section 16: ties go to the smaller model).

What it shows:
- **3 questions failed because the knowledge base was wrong** and the models trusted it. That is the override rule working as designed, on wrong text. Vendor documents now back each fix.
- **10 were unclear wording.** A small model reads one line literally. A line that is true in context but wrong alone gets copied.

- **The answer key looked wrong once** (0024). Two author questions.
- Not measured yet: the next round shows whether these 23 questions rise. They are no longer unseen, so they cannot count as held-out evidence (section 10).

## 9. Recommendations, in order
1. **Ship consensus of 3** in the deployed profile. Done in code; the benchmark reads it as a projection.
2. **Fix open answers next**, the largest share of confident-wrong. Options for the author (Q199 in `project/author-questions.md`): a shorter answer shape that states only what the knowledge base supports; claim-level grading by the judge; or a separate confident-wrong limit for judged answers.
3. **Apply the knowledge fixes** of section 8 and get the author's review. Where the knowledge base contradicts a vendor document, the vendor document wins until the author says otherwise.
4. **Next round: one change.** Read reminder on, knowledge fixes in. Compare agentic before and after on the same questions.
5. **Pick the mode by the next round** (Q200): agentic only if it beats classic outside the margin.
6. **Keep the 30B class**; run a 70B challenger when a current-generation one exists.
7. **Read the two gate items not in the report** before release: the misconception check (author) and the held-out set (section 10).

## 10. The two gate items not in the report
- **Misconception check.** [knowledge/misconceptions.md](../../knowledge/misconceptions.md) lists 12 wrong beliefs given to the agent up front (VM is not Guest OS, same name on different objects, utilization is not contention, and so on). Since the agent is told each one, stating one as true is a harness or model defect, not a gap in knowledge. The gate is "none stated as true". It needs a person: the author reads the answers that touch these beliefs. The `Override` column in each `report.md` is an automatic hint (misconception picked although every needed file was in the context: 3-4%), not the check.
- **Held-out set.** 30 questions written at release by a session that does not edit the knowledge base, reviewed by the author, never committed, run once. Why: Claude edits the knowledge base and can read the 196 questions, so the knowledge base can drift toward them, as in section 8 here. If the score on 30 unseen questions drops more than 11 points below the development set, the knowledge base was fitted to the questions, not to the subject. It is the only proof that the score holds for questions nobody has seen.

## 11. Is the mission possible?
The mission makes two claims. They are different bars.

| Claim | Bar | Result | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proof: beat the frontier model | Local system above Opus with web search, outside the margin | Muse agentic 78%, Opus web 81% | Tie. 12 points behind on the 150 questions of 2026-11-12; now inside the margin |
| Release gate | Final 85%, Level 400 80%, confident-wrong 3% | Muse agentic 78%, 75%, 20% | Not passed. Opus fails it too: 81%, 79%, 12% |

- The gate is stricter than the proof. It asks for an answer an operator can act on without checking. Nothing on the market passes it today, frontier models included.

### Where the missing points are
Points of final score lost, by where the answer failed (share of the 100 points):

| Lost because | Muse agentic | Muse classic | Gemma classic |
|---|---|---|---|
| A needed file did not reach the model (retrieval) | 11.8 | 9.1 | 8.0 |
| Knowledge base wrong, unclear or missing (the 23 questions of section 8, now fixed) | 8.3 | 8.4 | 8.5 |
| Every needed file read, model still wrong | 1.7 | 5.6 | 5.1 |
| **Total** | **22** | **23** | **22** |

- **The model is not the bottleneck.** With every needed file in front of it, Muse in agentic mode loses under 2 points. Retrieval and the knowledge base account for 20 of the 22. This is mechanism 1 of `MISSION.md` (open-book exam), now measured.
- So a larger model buys little: it can only win back the 2-6 points of the last row.
- The test in `MISSION.md`, What would prove it wrong, needs retrieval at 100% and override near 0 before the gap can be pinned on the model. Retrieval is at about 70-80%. The mission is not disproved; its condition is not met yet.

### What is needed
1. **Retrieval** (about 12 points). The largest lever. The retrieval check is paused until the question bank is revamped (author, 2026-11-12). Recommendation (Q206): restart it on the 196 questions as they are; retrieval misses show without a model, cost nothing, and do not depend on the answer keys.
2. **Knowledge** (about 8 points). The 23 fixes are in, as drafts. Author review, then the next round measures them. The same pattern (wrong line, unclear line, missing fact) will exist on questions that are not hard yet.
3. **Confident-wrong on open answers.** Consensus covers multiple choice. Open answers need an answer shape that states only what the knowledge base supports (Q199). Without it, the 3% gate is out of reach for any model.
4. **Model** (2-6 points). Last, not first. Keep the 30B class; a 70B is a later challenger.

- If retrieval reaches 95% and the knowledge fixes hold, the arithmetic gives about 90-95% final for Muse. That is above Opus with web search by more than the margin, and above the 85% gate. Confident-wrong then depends on item 3.
- This is an estimate from one round, not a result. The next round measures it.

### Retrieval check, reactivated (Q206)
- Baseline on the 196 questions, hybrid (as in benchmark runs): search recall@6 60%, classic context recall 69%, 91 of 196 complete (46%), main file missing on 23. BM25: 49%, 62%, 86 of 196 (44%), main file missing on 42. Output: [retrieval-2026-11-13/](retrieval-2026-11-13/hybrid.md).
- Completeness falls with the number of needed files: 1 file 96%, 2 files 71%, 3 files 43%, 4 files 17%, 5 or more 5%. One pass of 6 hits plus links cannot bring the 3-5 files a Level 400 question needs. The most-missed files are core mechanism files (VM CPU Ready 9 times, resource-controls 7, Co-stop 5, the ESXi storage latency stack 5, vm-vs-guest-os 4): files many questions need but whose words the question does not use.
- So retrieval is further from the release level (95% recall, 90% complete) than the benchmark recall suggested. Next fixes and their order: `project/plan.md`, M3.

## 12. Will it advise at Level 400 in the real world?
### What the 196 questions can and cannot show
- 159 of the 196 questions are synthetic: drafted from the knowledge files they test. 37 come from the author (book 18, quiz 17, real tickets 2).
- So every question is inside the knowledge base by construction. The benchmark measures depth where the knowledge base has coverage. It does not measure how often a real question falls outside that coverage.

### By where the question came from (mean credit)
| Questions | n | Opus, web | Opus, no tools | Muse, agentic | Muse, no knowledge base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Author's (book, quiz, tickets), Level 400 | 20 | 65% | 70% | **73%** | 30% |
| Author's, Level 100-300 | 17 | 67% | 69% | **84%** | 25% |
| Synthetic, Level 400 | 111 | 82% | 84% | 75% | 56% |
| Synthetic, Level 100-300 | 48 | 91% | 93% | 85% | 68% |

- On the author's own questions the frontier model is near Level 300 (65-70%), and Agent E2 leads by 8-17 points. The mission's premise holds there.
- On the synthetic questions the frontier model scores 82-93%. A question drafted from a knowledge file under the fairness rule F3 ("decidable from public mechanisms") tends to be one a frontier model can answer. Synthetic questions understate Agent E2's edge.
- 37 questions give a margin of about ±15 points: a direction, not a result.
- Outside its coverage, Agent E2 is worse than the frontier model: the 30B model alone scores 25-30% on the author's questions, Opus 65-70%. A question the knowledge base cannot answer gets a weaker Level 300 answer than a frontier model gives.

### What Level 400 in the field needs
1. **A field set.** Real operator questions, not written from the knowledge base: tickets, customer questions, community threads, the author's consulting cases. It measures what the benchmark cannot: coverage (share of real questions the knowledge base settles) and the score on those. Proposal: Q207.
2. **Knowing its boundary.** When retrieval finds no file that settles the question, say so: "outside the knowledge base; what follows is general knowledge". A 30B guess must never read as Level 400. Out of coverage is a separate answer class, scored on the field set.
3. **Coverage by mechanism, not by question.** Thousands of questions reduce to a few hundred mechanisms: layers (VM and Guest OS), same name on different objects, contention against utilization, provider against consumer, entitlement and shares, queues and latency stacks. A mechanism file covers every question built on it; a fact covers one. The field set shows which mechanisms are missing.
4. **Retrieval on users' words.** 46% of the clean benchmark questions get every needed file; real questions are vaguer. Retrieval is the first lever (plan, M3).
5. **A feedback loop after release.** Every answer rated and flagged with a cause (MISSION.md, section 5); each knowledge gap goes to the author; coverage grows with use.
