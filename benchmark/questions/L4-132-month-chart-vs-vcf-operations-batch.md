# A batch burst in the vCenter month export and in VCF Operations

## Prompt
- rpt-db-02, a 16-vCPU reporting VM with no CPU limit, sized to fit one NUMA node of its host, ran a batch for about 20 minutes last Tuesday night (2026-11-10, around 22:40 local time, GMT+08:00).
- The application owner exported [the VM's CPU Usage from vCenter, Last month](0370/rpt-db-02-cpu-usage-last-month.csv). Its row at 2026-11-10T14:00:00.000Z is the 2-hour point that covers the batch.
- From VCF Operations she exported the same VM for the same 2 hours, in 5-minute values: [CPU|Usage (%)](0370/rpt-db-02-vcf-cpu-usage.csv), [CPU|Contention (%)](0370/rpt-db-02-vcf-cpu-contention.csv), [CPU|Ready (%)](0370/rpt-db-02-vcf-cpu-ready.csv), [CPU|Co-stop (%)](0370/rpt-db-02-vcf-cpu-co-stop.csv) and [CPU|Overlap (%)](0370/rpt-db-02-vcf-cpu-overlap.csv).
- She says: "VCF Operations exaggerates the batch. vCenter shows a far lower number."

Which statements are correct?

- A. The vCenter 2-hour row for the batch averages the VM's Usage over its 2 hours, so the 20-minute batch is about a sixth of what it averages.
- B. For sizing rpt-db-02 over the coming months, the vCenter month export is the source to plan from.
- C. Each tool computes Usage its own way, so the two exports can never be set side by side.
- D. Contention is a VCF Operations calculation; vCenter has no such counter for the VM.
- E. In the VCF Operations exports, Contention during the batch equals Ready.
- F. Co-stop accounts for most of the distance between Contention and Ready during the batch.
- G. Contention rose during the batch, so the cluster needs another host.

## Likely wrong answer
E: Contention is just Ready under another name. Or C: the two tools compute Usage differently, so their numbers cannot be compared.

## Expected key points
- Answer: A, D, F.
- A is correct: the Last month interval keeps 2-hour points averaged from four 1 Week points; the 20 minutes of batch weigh a sixth. The 14:00Z row equals the mean of the 24 VCF Operations 5-minute Usage values (four batch points at 80-100%, twenty at 20-40%).
- B is wrong: vCenter is an administrative tool; its history is short and averaged (2-hour points here, which flatten the batch). Capacity is planned from VCF Operations.
- C is wrong: over-correction. Usage is a vSphere counter that VCF Operations passes through and means the same in both tools; the averaging window is what differs, and the mean of the 24 VCF Operations values gives the vCenter row.
- D is correct: Contention is a VCF Operations metric; the nearest vSphere counter is Latency, with another formula.
- E is wrong: reading. In the four batch rows CPU|Contention (%) is at least 2 points above CPU|Ready (%): it adds Co-stop, Overlap and a small Other Wait to Ready.
- F is correct: reading. Contention = Overlap + Co-stop + Ready + Other Wait (below 1%); in every batch row CPU|Co-stop (%) is more than two thirds of Contention minus Ready.
- G is wrong: wrong action. Contention on one VM does not size a cluster: it mixes Ready, Co-stop, Overlap and limits of one VM, and it cannot measure ESXi or cluster performance. Read Ready across the host's and the cluster's VMs, and the VM's width, first.

## Reasoning path
1. The 2-hour row averages 24 five-minute values: the batch is a sixth of it (A).
2. Subtract Ready from Contention during the batch and compare the rest with Co-stop.
3. No collection fault: interval, rollup and formula explain both gaps.
