# A Ready burst in the Real-time and Last day exports

## Prompt
- The owner of erp-app-03, an 8-vCPU VM, reports a short freeze about 45 minutes ago.
- The vCenter keeps statistics level 1 (the default) for all intervals.
- You export the VM's CPU from vCenter: [the Real-time export](0369/erp-app-03-cpu-real-time.csv) and [the Last day export](0369/erp-app-03-cpu-last-day.csv).

Which statements are correct?

- A. The highest Real-time Ready sample puts each vCPU in Ready for about 2-3% of that sample.
- B. The Real-time peak puts each vCPU in Ready about a fifth of that sample.
- C. A Last day row of Ready adds up the 15 Real-time samples of its 5 minutes.
- D. The Last day Ready row holding the burst is below 0.5% of the time per vCPU.
- E. Each Last day Usage row adds up the 15 Real-time Usage samples of its 5 minutes.
- F. Several Real-time samples in the same 5 minutes pass 3,000 ms of Ready.
- G. The Last day Ready row holding the burst reads higher in ms than the Real-time peak.
- H. Raise the vCenter statistics level to 4 so that the Last day export keeps the burst at full height.
- I. Judge Ready from the Last day export: a single 20-second sample is mostly noise.

## Answers
A, C, D, G

## Explanation
- A is correct: Ready (ms) is summed over the 8 vCPUs; one 20-second sample holds 8 x 20,000 = 160,000 ms. The highest `Ready for erp-app-03` value in the Real-time export, 3,500-4,500 ms, is about 2.2-2.8% per vCPU.
- B is wrong: the misconception. 20,000 ms is the base of one vCPU; the VM value is the sum of eight.
- C is correct: Ready is a summation counter; the rollup sums the 15 samples (300,000 ms per vCPU). In the exports each Last day Ready row equals the sum of its 15 Real-time samples, while the Usage row is their mean.
- D is correct: the row holding the burst is below 10,000 ms; against 8 x 300,000 = 2,400,000 ms it is at most about 0.4%.
- E is wrong: Usage has an average rollup: each Last day Usage row is the mean of its 15 samples (it stays in the range of the Real-time values), while Ready, a summation counter, is their sum.
- F is wrong: reading. One sample stands out; the other 14 in those 5 minutes stay below 400 ms.
- G is correct: a sum of 15 samples exceeds its largest sample, while its per-vCPU percentage is far lower.
- H is wrong: wrong action. A level applies from the moment it is set, so the past burst cannot be rebuilt; and Ready is stored as a summation: a higher level adds minimum and maximum rollups of aggregated values, not the raw 20-second samples, so the 5-minute row stays diluted.
- I is wrong: over-correction. Ready is read at the 20-second sample: a wait that lasts seconds shows there and is diluted in 5-minute rows.
- Misconception: B: 4,000 of 20,000 ms puts the VM in Ready a fifth of the time. Or E: every 5-minute row adds up its samples, whatever the counter.

## Reasoning path
1. Divide the Real-time peak by 20,000 ms x 8 vCPUs.
2. Divide the Last day row by 300,000 ms x 8 vCPUs.
3. Compare: the burst is diluted in the 5-minute row.
